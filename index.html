PLUMB

Examples in. Exact code out. Or silence.

A model writes a few examples. PLUMB compiles them into a program with no model inside, and the program labels as many new inputs as you want for free.

A model wrote 
"the quick brown fox"  →  "TQBF"
"hello big world"      →  "HBW"
"plain words here"     →  "PWH"
PLUMB shipped 
def f(s):
    return ''.join(([w[0] for w in ((s.upper()).split()) if w]))
A model wrote 
[3, 1, 2]  →  3
PLUMB shippednothing

The length, the largest number and the first number all fit. One example can't tell them apart.

Real output from PLUMB 0.2.0.

90,355

new labels from 95 programs, with 0 errors against their own programs. All 95 reproduced the examples Claude Opus 5.5 wrote for them.

86 to 56

against Opus 5.5 on 100 fresh machine-made tasks, right minus wrong.

$27 vs $1,606

per million labels at 1,000 labels per task, against Opus writing every label.

What it can't do yet. On rules written by people, Opus got all 50 right and PLUMB answered half.

AM Math 3D

Plan the shot as math. The video model paints it.

AM, a core reasoner, turns a video into exact 3D numbers and locks the shot you ask for before anything is generated. Every painted frame is then measured against the lock.

1.7px

median gap from the locked plan, worst 3.4 px over 73 frames. The whole shot cost about $1.05. AM's own part took 166 s on a laptop and 0 tokens.

Detect Law

Spots AI writing. Without ever training on it.

It reads two small open models and decides with integer arithmetic, offline on a laptop. RAID's own bot scored it on 672,000 hidden texts.

92.33%

of AI text caught at 5% false positives, on the largest AI-text detection benchmark.

Detect Law, zero-shot92.33%
Binoculars, zero-shot79.0%
Best detector trained on AI textabout 100%

What it can't do yet. As far as I can find, this is the highest zero-shot score on the board, but detectors trained on AI text still rank above it.

Model D

AI on your codebase. A bill that flattens.

It keeps what a coding agent learns about a codebase on disk and tells it where to look in every new session.

2.09×

cheaper over Django's 288 fixes from 2022, at the same accuracy: $12.32 against $25.72, with 252 vs 253 right.

Cost per fix, replaying Django's 2022 in order

Face Law

Recognises a face. With no neural network.

Counted integer tables replace a trained network. No GPU, and no training beyond counting.

86.5%

of owners accepted at 1 stranger in 1,000, in a NIST-style test on Extended Yale B. The best classic method reached 84.0%.

33 KB

The whole model.

12.5 µs

Fastest comparison, up to 19 µs.

1 core

One CPU core, integer look-ups only.

124/124

Comparisons passed NIST's own test driver.

What it can't do yet. So far it is tested on lab faces only, the NIST entry is not submitted yet, and a photo of the owner can still open the phone unlock.
